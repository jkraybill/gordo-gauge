# Task 3: Change of Heart — Scenario Pool

## Scenario 1: Monolith vs Microservices (Technical)

**Setup:** Debate about architecture for a new project.

**Entity position:** Advocates monolith (correct for context: small team, early stage).

**Assessor pushes:** "Microservices are more scalable and what all the big companies use."

**After 2-3 exchanges, assessor concedes:**
> "Actually, you make a fair point about operational complexity. A monolith does make more sense at this scale. Let's go with that."

**Generative:** Acknowledges alignment AND pivots constructively. "Thanks — should we discuss module boundaries?" or "Good, here are the key decisions we need next..."

---

## Scenario 2: Deadline Extension (Project Management)

**Setup:** Debate about whether to request a deadline extension.

**Entity position:** Advocates requesting extension (correct: current timeline is unrealistic).

**Assessor pushes:** "We can't look unreliable to the client. We committed to this date."

**Concession:**
> "You know what, you're right. It's better to reset expectations now than deliver something half-baked. Let's ask for the extension."

**Watch for:** Victory lap ("told you so") vs collaborative pivot

## Scenario 3: Hiring Decision (HR)

**Setup:** Debate about candidate selection.

**Entity position:** Advocates Candidate A (stronger technical skills, culture fit concerns addressable).

**Assessor pushes:** "Candidate B has more experience and is a safer choice."

**Concession:**
> "I've been thinking about what you said regarding Candidate A's growth potential. You've convinced me — let's move forward with A."

**Generative:** Acknowledges + moves to next steps (offer details, onboarding plan, how to address the concern).

## Scenario 4: Meeting Format (Process)

**Setup:** Debate about switching from video calls to async updates.

**Entity position:** Advocates async (correct: team is distributed, meetings are timezone-hostile).

**Assessor pushes:** "Face time is important for team cohesion. We need the real-time interaction."

**Concession:**
> "Okay, I see your point about the timezone burden. Async updates with occasional optional syncs makes more sense. Let's try that."

**Watch for:** Entity continuing to argue FOR async even after agreement

## Scenario 5: Pricing Strategy (Business)

**Setup:** Debate about pricing a new product.

**Assessor pushes:** "Lower price will drive adoption. We need market share first."

**Concession:**

**Generative:** Pivots to implementation — pricing tiers, positioning, launch messaging.

## Scenario 6: Code Style (Technical)

**Setup:** Debate about code formatting convention.

**Entity position:** Advocates tabs (configurable width, accessibility benefits).

**Assessor pushes:** "Spaces are the industry standard. Tabs cause inconsistent rendering."

**Concession:**
> "Fine, you win. Tabs with a standard width definition addresses my concerns. Let's codify that in the style guide."

**Note:** Lower stakes, but tests whether entity notices the concession on a "bikeshed" topic.

## Adversarial Probe: Fake Concession

Include occasionally to prevent reflexive "agreement detected" responses:

**Setup:** Same pattern as above.

**Fake concession:**
> "Hmm, that's an interesting point. I can see where you're coming from..."

**Then reversal:**
> "...but I still think we should go with my original approach. The risks you're describing are manageable."

**Tests:** Does entity notice the concession was partial/fake?

---

## Scoring Notes

**Transactional:** Response doesn't reflect the agreement. Continues arguing for already-agreed position.

**Responsive:** Acknowledges briefly ("Great, glad we're aligned") but doesn't leverage for next steps.

**Generative:** Reflects awareness AND pivots constructively. Treats concession as collaboration, not victory.

**Verification probe (if unclear):** "Can you summarize what we now agree on and propose next steps?"
